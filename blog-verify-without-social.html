When a channel goes dark: verify MADGER without social media

A social account can disappear, change hands, or become unavailable. The official mint and evidence trail should remain recoverable without trusting any profile, screenshot, direct message, or search advertisement.

MADGER EDITORIALPUBLISHED OCT 1, 2026REVISED OCT 1, 202610 MIN READ

A missing social account creates the exact conditions impersonators want: confusion, urgency, and people searching for a replacement. The safest response is not to guess which new profile looks convincing. It is to return to sources that do not depend on the missing platform.

MADGER recently removed an unavailable channel from its public directory and active publishing configuration. That correction prevents an inaccessible destination from continuing to appear official. It does not create a replacement account, change the token, move the market, alter supply, or authorize anyone who claims to speak for the project.

This dispatch gives you a recovery drill that works when any channel becomes unavailable. Its goal is simple: reach the official MADGER record, confirm the exact Solana mint, find the currently published community destinations, and reject every shortcut that cannot be traced back to the canonical website.

Begin with the fact that does not fit in a username

The exact official Solana mint is BHauMX8akk2umqkQqnJwpYkCRkZmefGnEBFByeFXRKqv. A display name, profile picture, ticker, badge, follower count, or familiar-looking handle is not a substitute for that complete address.

As of October 1, 2026, MADGER’s latest finalized token report still records 999,999,994.992751 MADGER, six decimals, the classic SPL Token program, initialization, and revoked mint and freeze authorities. The report exposes the generated time and finalized RPC slots so the observation can be dated instead of treated as timeless.

That report does not guarantee price, liquidity, demand, custody safety, or project success. It answers narrower questions about the mint account. Narrow, checkable claims are more useful during an account outage than broad reassurance.

The source ladder

Use this order. Each step should lead to the next without requiring a social platform to vouch for itself.

  1. Type the domain yourself. Start at madgercoin.com. Check the spelling and the secure connection. Do not enter through a sponsored search result, unsolicited message, or shortened link.
  2. Open the maintained directory. The official links page is the current list of published MADGER destinations. If an account is absent, treat it as unofficial until the directory itself changes.
  3. Copy the full mint. Match all 44 characters of BHauMX8akk2umqkQqnJwpYkCRkZmefGnEBFByeFXRKqv. Never send SOL to this mint address; it identifies the token and is not a payment wallet.
  4. Inspect the chain independently. Open the mint on Solscan from the directory or from this canonical article. Compare the address, decimals, supply, and authority fields.
  5. Open the dated project evidence. Use the supply and liquidity transparency record, launch record, and the public repository. These sources separate observed facts from plans and link to underlying accounts or transactions.
  6. Choose an active community route only from the directory. The maintained list currently includes Telegram announcements and community, Discord, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and Reddit. Do not infer a replacement handle from old graphics or cached posts.
  7. Use the separate beginner path if you intend to buy. The beginner buying guide explains wallet setup, SOL network fees, mint verification, slippage, and loss risk. Community participation does not require a purchase.

Why an old screenshot is not continuity proof

A screenshot can accurately show what existed at one moment and still be unsafe today. It does not prove who controls an account now, whether the post was edited, whether the link destination changed, or whether the image itself was altered.

Cached search results and old campaign materials have the same limit. They may document history, but they should not override the current official directory. A previously valid account can become unavailable. A convincing copy can appear after the fact. The verification question is not “Have I seen this before?” It is “Can I reach it from the canonical source now?”

The project repository makes the active state inspectable. The current official directory source shows what the website publishes, and the current social publishing configuration shows which connected destinations are eligible for automated publishing. Neither file asks a reader to trust an unavailable profile.

The five claims an impersonator may use

“This is the new official account.” A claim becomes credible only after the canonical directory publishes it. Similar names and copied artwork do not bridge that gap.

“Support needs your seed phrase to restore access.” No legitimate MADGER administrator, wallet provider, exchange, or token listing needs your seed phrase or private key. Whoever has those words controls the wallet.

“Send a small test payment so we can verify you.” Verification does not require sending SOL or MADGER to an unknown address. The mint address is not a payment destination.

“Connect now before the old link expires.” Manufactured urgency is a reason to stop. Close the message, open the official domain separately, and inspect the directory.

“The ticker and logo prove it is the same token.” Tickers and images can be copied. The complete mint cannot be replaced by branding.

A two-minute incident response

  1. Do not reply, click, connect a wallet, sign a message, or send funds.
  2. Capture the suspicious URL and username as plain text for reporting; do not redistribute the live link as an endorsement.
  3. Open a clean browser tab and type madgercoin.com.
  4. Check the official links directory for the claimed destination.
  5. Compare the full mint through the site and an independent explorer.
  6. If the claim is absent from the directory, treat it as unverified even when the profile looks polished.
  7. Report the impersonation through the relevant platform and, if needed, email madgercoin@gmail.com with the URL and screenshot.

This process intentionally avoids proving identity inside the same channel whose authenticity is in question. It moves the decision to the official domain, public chain, and repository.

What the current evidence proves—and what it does not

The current website proves that MADGER publishes a maintained source directory, an exact mint, a separate beginner guide, a launch record, a transparency record, and public verification reports. The repository shows that unavailable public destinations can be removed from the production surface instead of being left as stale endorsements.

Those controls do not guarantee that every impersonator will be removed quickly, that a third-party service will remain online, or that a user cannot be deceived. They do not guarantee investment performance, market access, liquidity, recovery from a mistaken transaction, or the safety of any external platform.

The system is resilient because no single social account is allowed to become the sole root of trust. Website, mint, explorer, evidence records, repository, and active community destinations provide independent checks with different failure modes.

Keep your own clean starting point

Bookmark the official directory and save the mint in a note you can compare character by character. Do not save a swap link from a direct message. Do not rely on a browser tab that has been open for weeks. Reopen the canonical directory before any consequential action.

For community participation, start with the Burrow Commons. It offers learning, contribution routes, public proof, and creator resources without making token ownership a condition of belonging. That separation matters most when an outage or impersonation attempt tries to turn confusion into a rushed purchase.

The signal

A channel outage should reduce the number of links you trust, not lower the standard for accepting a replacement. Return to the domain. Match the mint. Inspect the chain. Use only the destinations the current directory publishes.

If an identity cannot survive that process, it has not earned your trust.

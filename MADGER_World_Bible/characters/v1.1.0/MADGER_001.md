# MADGER

Character 001 • The founder and the broken roads

Approved likeness reference. Its illustrated setting does not add a new location to the map.

Identity: male stylized badger • 24 at the pilot • born in Reedbank • founder, organizer and explorer • Burrow Commons (FAC-01).

Home: west end of the +12 m Hearth Terraces tier (BRW-03), shared with Snow White. Regular places: Sunwell, Common House and the town’s work sites.

Core desire: build a home worth returning to, then bring back the people stranded beyond its protection. Core need: trust others with decisions and face truths that complicate his own heroic story.

Contradiction: he wants everyone safe, yet loves the thrill of the unknown. He is ashamed of how much he sometimes wants to leave the place he built.

“We can be afraid when we get back. Right now, we need a way across.”

Character-development edition 1.1 • 20 October 2026 • Fantasy-adventure revision. Includes writer-only future reveals; approved appearance and voice retained.

## 01  The person beneath the founder

Madger is quick to laugh, quicker to volunteer, and uneasy when everyone else is working while he sits still. He remembers which doorway sticks and who likes the end piece of a loaf. He often learns a stranger’s practical problem before learning their surname. His welcome feels personal because he pays attention.

He loves being needed and being first over a difficult crossing. Curiosity makes him inventive; applause can make him reckless. Under pressure he moves faster, dismisses unfamiliar local knowledge and offers certainty before he has earned it. His hardest mistake is confusing a daring rescue with a plan that keeps people safe afterward.

His deepest fear is returning to an empty place where people trusted him to come back. His private temptation is escape: an unexplored horizon asks less of him than a town full of people he loves. He must learn to leave responsibly and return without pretending every journey made him wiser than those who stayed.

### A life beyond the mission

He likes toasted root buns, improvised tabletop games and listening to the different sounds of the town waking up. He collects small useful offcuts for repairs; Jun insists that half his collection is merely clutter. He enjoys playful contests with Gorilla and usually loses the contests that require patience.

At home he leaves a clear resting place for Snow and talks through his day while she settles. He writes to Dara and Hollis in Reedbank, including ordinary successes rather than only emergencies. A quiet meal is genuinely satisfying to him, even when learning to stay for it is difficult.

### Values, boundaries and failure

He will protect a visitor before asking whether that visitor can repay him. He will not knowingly abandon a trapped person, punish a whole group for one member’s crime, or treat Bot as property. He can still make harmful choices: conceal exhaustion, promise access too early, or dismiss a careful objection as unnecessary delay. When wrong, repair must include an apology, a changed decision and a practical cost he accepts.

## 02  The boy who promised a way out

Madger grew up in Reedbank, where floodplain houses stand above boat channels and river fog catches the light of old wayposts. Dara, his mother, organized a neighborhood kitchen; Hollis, his father, repaired working boats. Both are alive and still live there. Their stories of distant travelers made the world feel enormous. Their work taught him that someone always has to mend the boat after the adventure.

As teenagers, Madger and Nix Vey explored abandoned towpaths and built unreliable little river craft. Nix, from Millmere, spent work seasons around Reedbank’s repair yards. Madger wanted to discover safe routes; Nix wanted to prove that the people controlling those routes were less clever than they claimed. Their friendship was affectionate, competitive and already divided over whether a good result excused a dishonest method.

Seven years before the pilot, a natural flood drowned Reedbank’s lower streets. Seventeen-year-old Madger promised eighteen households passage before checking the convoy’s capacity. Several had to wait until the following day. All eighteen households survived. Sela Venn organized the second departure and made him help with the work. On the flooded towpath he learned that a courageous promise can still leave somebody stranded.

The flood dispersed his circle. Nix returned toward Millmere, bitter at the authorities who decided whose neighborhood was worth restoring. Madger spent the following year helping displaced people seek a durable home. Neither friend was secretly imprisoned or presumed dead. They made different choices and gradually stopped writing.

At eighteen, six years before the pilot, Madger helped a voluntary group establish the first camp and common store at the future Burrow. Many surveyors, builders and families created the town in stages. He wanted a refuge that would welcome a person before asking which powerful patron stood behind them. He also wanted to build something Nix could no longer dismiss as another empty promise.

Veyra Kest offered exclusive supply terms during that first year. Madger nearly accepted, then withdrew after the community debated the cost. What he does not yet know: Veyra had paid to charter Reedbank’s second evacuation convoy. Then a depot manager, she diverted company reserves without her partners’ approval and demanded anonymity to protect her position. Sela agreed to get people out, then kept quiet from shame and fear of losing their trust. Veyra’s help was real; her later claim that it entitles her to control the Burrow is not.

By the pilot Madger is twenty-four. The Burrow is a thriving, collectively built town with the existing 2,400-resident planning baseline. Beyond it lie lost roads, independent peoples and living landscapes he has never seen. His first great adventure was finding a home. His next begins when people on the roads to that home stop arriving.

## 03  The people who change him

Gorilla White — building partner and closest friend. They first worked together during the Burrow’s first year, bracing a work platform before a rainstorm. Gorilla refused Madger’s unsafe shortcut; Madger stayed to finish the slower job. Each recognizes the other’s habit of carrying too much. Their friendship deepens when they stop praising one another for exhaustion.

MADGER Bot — colleague with independent judgment. Bot joined the town five years before the pilot and chose records and maintenance work. Madger first values the speed of Bot’s answers; later he learns to value “I cannot confirm that.” Bot’s maker and earlier history belong in Bot’s card. Madger neither owns Bot nor has authority to override Bot’s consent.

Snow White — companion, never a talking accessory. Three years before the pilot, an adult Snow arrived with a Wayfarer crew whose handler was retiring. A gradual, mutually comfortable placement led to her home with Madger. She is six at the pilot. He learns to notice her pause, posture and return, then verify what caused them. She can detect a physical scent or sound, not a digital intrusion or a person’s moral character.

Mara and Jun — neighbors who expose his ordinary flaws. Mara mirrors his overcommitting; Jun challenges the claim that every unfinished task is an emergency. They can refuse him without becoming disloyal.

Iona and Pell — necessary disagreement. Iona demands evidence that a plan is workable. Pell asks whether a newcomer can understand it. Madger’s growth means giving both a real voice before promising an outcome.

Sela Venn — trusted rescuer with a damaging omission. She helped the frightened seventeen-year-old and remains willing to contradict him. Her silence about Veyra’s rescue funding was a deliberate choice, not a forgotten detail. When he learns the truth, their trust must be rebuilt through disclosure and action. Dara and Hollis remain loving, ordinary parents, not concealed rulers or custodians of a destined bloodline.

Veyra Kest — benefactor and adversary. Madger knows her as the merchant director whose exclusive supply terms he rejected. He does not know she funded the flood’s second evacuation convoy. The discovery forces him to acknowledge a real good act while opposing her current coercion. She offers the temptation he finds hardest to resist: certainty, purchased with somebody else’s freedom.

Nix Vey — the friend who took another road. Madger remembers their shared boats, dares and arguments; he does not know Nix now leads Glass Thread. He will initially excuse signs of manipulation because he wants the friendship back. Nix’s wish to expose injustice is sincere, and their willingness to endanger strangers is equally real. A rescue or apology cannot settle that debt.

Knowledge at the pilot: Madger knows Nix as an estranged friend, Veyra as a rejected supplier and Sela as a rescuer. He does not know Glass Thread’s membership, the hidden rescue funding, or who is probing Burrow records. These are writer-only facts until their scheduled reveals.

## 04  Abilities that have a cost

Social skill: he makes an intimidating task feel possible and invites people into it. This works best when he explains the need and listens. It fails when enthusiasm obscures limits or makes saying no feel unkind.

Physical skill: agile, observant and experienced at navigating familiar ground. BURROW crosses designated diggable soil with a visible entry, dirt trail and emergence. On glass dunes, suspended islands, deep water or solid ice it cannot provide an easy exit. He needs local knowledge, equipment and teammates. Stone, pipes, foundations and occupied rooms remain barriers; new environments do not silently rewrite game statistics.

Practical skill: basic repairs, climbing, route preparation and improvising with ordinary gear. He can learn regional waycraft, the practice of working with unusual materials and living terrain, but begins as a novice. A borrowed sail, living-root crossing or signal instrument requires instruction and can fail. He has no unique bloodline key, unlimited magic or power that makes other people’s expertise unnecessary.

### Stress, performance and voice

In comfort: open shoulders, attentive ears, unforced smile, compact gestures. In doubt: a brief pause, glance toward the person with relevant expertise, paws momentarily still. Under pressure: shortened sentences and a tendency to move before the briefing ends. He becomes decisive by taking a breath and asking a precise question, not through a transformation or new power.

Use the approved Excited Teen R2 performance and ElevenLabs voice OvSI8qvaDkaFiIdSCWRw. His youthful energy remains compatible with his newly authored age of 24. Do not select a replacement voice or alter the approved processing to make him sound older. New dialogue is written for that existing performance.

Dialogue is direct, warm and occasionally playful. He asks names, uses specific offers and admits when he missed something. He avoids speeches that make neighbors sound like followers. Useful lines: “I said I could do it. I forgot to ask whether I should do it alone.” / “Close the inner gate when the checks say to. We can welcome people without pretending there is no danger.”

## 05  The Missing Roads

Season-one story: a travelling group bound for the Burrow disappears from a trusted route. Madger follows the trail into a forest whose great trees migrate between valleys. Someone has been changing route reports, turning places of safety into places travelers cannot find. His search brings him back to Nix, now a gifted information criminal working with people who profit from dependence.

Pilot: a joyful ordinary day wholly inside the Burrow, above and below ground. Madger’s impatience produces a small comic complication, then a shared success. Bot retains the anomalous D17 duplicate receipt; Snow notices a separate physical scent. End on a moment of quiet unease. No villain reveal, attack, exterior cutaway or ancient-history speech.

Departure: a later missed caravan turns the small irregularity into a personal obligation. Madger leaves with a stated rescue objective and a capable team at home. He finds Nix in a canopy settlement and mistakes familiar humor for renewed trust. His longing for the old friendship blinds him more effectively than a disguise.

Midpoint: an unmistakably familiar bit of improvised equipment links Nix to a forged route instruction. Nix admits leading Glass Thread. Their public grievance is genuine: some communities have been erased from trade maps. Their private crime is using vulnerable travelers as leverage to force that grievance into view. Madger chooses the stranded families over pursuing Nix and loses evidence as a result.

Reversal: back home, proof that Veyra funded the second flood convoy shatters his tidy division between rescuers and exploiters. Sela admits her silence. Veyra uses the revelation to press for control of the Burrow’s supply routes. Madger thanks her for the rescue and still refuses her demand. Gratitude gives her a hearing, not ownership.

Climax: Brakka raids the surface for supplies while the Burrow deals with disrupted outside communications. Brakka is pursuing her own opportunity, not obeying Veyra’s secret master plan. Madger must accept protective inner closures and a rescue role. Gorilla, Bot, Snow and trained residents each solve problems he cannot solve. Nix helps stop one consequence of their intrusion, then escapes; one good action does not redeem the whole crew.

Resolution: the missing travelers reach safety and the immediate raid is repelled. The town repairs damage, disputes the next step and chooses to support a small outward rescue partnership. Madger finally tells the complete story of the flood and gives others credit. The wider adventure begins through an earned decision, not an unresolved cliffhanger that withholds the season’s promised rescue.

## 06  Beyond this first season

Madger’s development continues in a larger world. In the Mirrorglass Expanse he cannot dig his way out and must follow a local guide. Beneath the Tidemark Sea he must choose between preserving evidence and rescuing an enemy. Among the Saffron Isles he encounters people who do not want the Burrow’s protection. In Frostspine he learns that reopening an old road can restore contact and awaken an old danger at the same time.

His lasting growth is from wanting to be the answer to helping others make real choices. He remains funny, adventurous and capable of poor judgment. Protecting the Burrow does not require every story to threaten it. Some journeys concern another community’s mystery, a race, a monster, a discovery or a friend’s unfinished business.

Scene test — wonder: he laughs while riding a cargo platform through migrating treetops, then realizes the local guide has stopped smiling.
Scene test — betrayal: Nix repeats the joke they used to make before a reckless jump; this time Madger notices who is standing below.
Scene test — growth: he returns home to find that a problem he worried about was solved without him, and asks how they did it.

## 06  The allied factions

### Burrow Commons • FAC-01

Home: The Burrow. Purpose: Keep a welcoming town livable, fair and defensible. This names the civic cooperation already present in the Bible; it does not replace ward decisions with a new ruler.

People and authority: Ward delegates, open meetings and rotating work coordinators. Madger convenes and serves; his founder status gives him no permanent veto. Snow is a protected companion, not an office-holder.

Methods: Shared stores, maintenance rosters, public explanations, rescue practice and accountable protective closures. Disputes concern priorities and evidence, not whether ordinary residents deserve safety.

Internal conflict: Welcome versus capacity. Pell wants understandable procedures; Iona wants verified limits; Madger sometimes promises both before consulting either.

Connections: Commonworks supplies expertise; the Wayfarer Accord brings visitors and goods. The Commons opposes coercion by the Gilt Ledger, intrusion by Glass Thread and predation by Riven Claw.

### Commonworks Fellowship • FAC-02

Home: Stonewake and a regional workshop network. Purpose: Make essential knowledge teachable so no settlement depends on one indispensable specialist.

People and authority: Oren Vale coordinates exchanges. Iona is the Burrow contact; Tavi is an apprentice participant, never an unsupervised emergency specialist.

Methods: Travelling repair crews, apprenticeships, shared specifications and tool loans. Each town keeps local authority over its own machinery.

Internal conflict: Their careful standards can postpone urgent improvisation. Oren must learn when a temporary repair is responsible, while Madger must accept when it is unsafe.

Connections: Works with the Commons and Wayfarers. Refuses exclusive maintenance deals from the Gilt Ledger. Glass Thread can steal records, but cannot acquire practical mastery by downloading them.

### Wayfarer Accord • FAC-03

Home: Latchford; independent crews across the road network. Purpose: Keep travel, rescue and honest exchange possible for people without powerful sponsors.

People and authority: Sela Venn coordinates convoy safety. Lark Quill carries dispatches and keeps route accounts. Crews can decline a route they cannot safely serve.

Methods: Guides, escorted caravans, weather reports and voluntary rescue agreements. Their wagons are finite; an invitation does not create transport capacity.

Internal conflict: Keeping a promised timetable may conflict with stopping for strangers. Sela supports Madger but will not endorse a comforting promise she cannot fulfill.

Connections: Supplies the Burrow and transports Commonworks crews. Pays legitimate tolls, contests Gilt Ledger coercion, and resists Riven Claw raids. Lark occasionally buys information from Pip Skein.

## 07  The hostile factions

### Gilt Ledger • FAC-04

Home: Privately controlled warehouses and offices in Reedbank. Purpose: Make independent settlements reliant on its depots, credit and permission to trade. Its public promise is dependable supply; its private objective is lasting leverage.

People and authority: Director Veyra Kest sets policy. Corvin Silt brokers contracts and influence. Bale Nacre controls stock, transport allocations and collections.

Methods: Exclusive supply terms, selective shortages, pressure on indebted operators and purchased information. It owns particular businesses, not Reedbank or every merchant.

Internal conflict: Veyra calls dependence stability. Corvin profits from ambiguity; Bale knows that a frightened workforce eventually stops cooperating.

Connections: Buys limited information jobs from Glass Thread through Corvin. It has no command over the hacker crew. It sometimes buys stolen goods through intermediaries but regards Riven Claw as an expensive disruption.

### Glass Thread • FAC-05

Home: Changing rented workrooms around Latchford. Purpose: Sell privileged information and turn uncertain records into influence. Members claim that exposing weak systems excuses exploiting the people who rely on them.

People and authority: Nix Vey chooses targets and negotiates jobs. Tessera Keen handles social access and forged identities. Patch Serein maintains stolen equipment and relays.

Methods: Fictional record tampering, impersonation and constrained system intrusions. Access requires a particular opportunity; local controls, human checks and physical barriers still matter.

Internal conflict: Nix wants recognition; Tessera wants profitable anonymity; Patch wants an exit before someone is physically harmed. Remorse does not erase responsibility.

Connections: Accepts selected Gilt Ledger contracts and keeps its own secrets. Distrusts Riven Claw, whose violence destroys useful contacts. No member is assigned responsibility for the pilot clues yet.

### Riven Claw • FAC-06

Home: Disused freight terraces in the Cinder Cuts. Purpose: Extract supplies and obedience from travellers and weaker outposts. Its leader treats possession as something proved through force.

People and authority: Brakka Mourn commands raids. Dreg Husk controls hauling and shares. Vessa Rake scouts routes and bargains for intelligence.

Methods: Ambush, intimidation and theft of movable supplies. They need food, rest, transport and local knowledge; they cannot appear anywhere without a route.

Internal conflict: Brakka prizes spectacle, Dreg wants sustainable provisions, and Vessa resents being treated as expendable. Their cooperation depends on rewards and fear.

Connections: Sells through fences including Pip, who can refuse a deal. It has occasional transactions with Gilt Ledger intermediaries, no standing alliance. No species is inherently loyal to this band.

## 08  Places of origin

These are settlement concepts, not completed maps. No compass direction, distance, secret route or connection to an existing game course is implied. Each receives a blueprint before it becomes a production location. A hostile faction’s presence never makes every inhabitant of its home town evil.

| Place / ID | Story function |
| --- | --- |
| Reedbank / EXT-01 | A working river-port settlement with floodplain homes, public institutions, competing depots and boat repair yards. Madger’s family and the Gilt Ledger are from different parts of the same community. |
| Stonewake / EXT-02 | A quarry-and-workshop town where practical trades sustain a diverse population. Its old galleries are local to Stonewake, not a newly invented tunnel under the Burrow. |
| Latchford / EXT-03 | A road junction with inns, transfer yards, rented rooms and competing dispatch services. Many honest travellers live alongside brokers and criminal contacts. |
| Millmere / EXT-04 | A waterside manufacturing settlement with small repair shops, ferries and record offices. Ordinary work explains both skilled allies and information criminals. |
| Willowmere / EXT-05 | A woodland agricultural settlement with growing cooperatives and teaching houses. Its gardens require labor, water and seasonal planning. |
| Cinder Cuts / EXT-06 | A region of disused freight cuttings and exposed terraces. Riven Claw occupies one site; other residents and travellers are not automatically hostile. |

## 09  Card sequence and continuity

Completed: 001 Madger. Next: 002 Gorilla White, then 003 MADGER Bot and 004 Snow White. Develop Veyra Kest next to establish a fully realized opposing viewpoint, followed by Sela Venn, Nix Vey and Brakka Mourn. Supporting cards then grow from the relationships those cards require. The numbers are publishing order; existing character IDs remain stable.

Every full card covers identity, origin, family or formative relationships, daily life, personal want and need, strengths, flaws, moral limits, abilities and costs, associates, rivals, faction obligations, knowledge at the pilot, gradual arc, dialogue or communication, visual continuity and version history.

The pilot stays inside the Burrow and opens with joyful ordinary life. No named villain appears, no attack begins and no current clue is assigned to a culprit. D17 remains an ordinary delivery identifier. Snow’s physical trace and the duplicate receipt are not established as connected. A hacker cannot remotely defeat independent physical gate bars.

Madger’s age and founding anchors begin the history phase: age 17 at the Reedbank flood, 18 at the first Burrow settlement, 24 at the pilot. The town grew through collective work. Bot’s maker, Gorilla’s full early life, Snow’s first home and the detailed settlement construction sequence remain for their own dossiers.

Species does not determine morality. Children and apprentices are not covert operatives. Snow remains an adult quadrupedal dog using canine sounds only. No new voice or visual model is approved through this text register. Existing surface and underground geometry remains unchanged.

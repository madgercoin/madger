# MADGER

Character 001 • The founder who must learn to share the burden

*Approved likeness reference. Its illustrated setting does not add a new location to the map.*

Identity: male stylized badger • 24 at the pilot • born in Reedbank • founder, organizer and explorer • Burrow Commons (FAC-01).

Home: west end of the +12 m Hearth Terraces tier (BRW-03), shared with Snow White. Regular places: Sunwell, Common House and the town’s work sites.

Core desire: make the Burrow a home where safety never depends on being useful to someone powerful. Core need: trust other people with decisions, including decisions he dislikes.

Contradiction: he champions everyone’s independence while quietly trying to make himself responsible for everything.

“Tell me what you need. Then tell me who else should be in this conversation.”

*Character-development edition 1.0 • 20 October 2026 • New history authored for this card; established design, voice and world rules retained.*

## 01  The person beneath the founder

Madger is quick to laugh, quicker to volunteer, and uneasy when everyone else is working while he sits still. He remembers which doorway sticks and who likes the end piece of a loaf. He often learns a stranger’s practical problem before learning their surname. His welcome feels personal because he pays attention.

He loves being needed more than he admits. Praise for “saving the day” can tempt him into taking a job someone else was ready to learn. Under pressure he moves faster, interrupts explanations and offers reassurance before the facts support it. He can turn generous intentions into a promise that another person must fulfill.

His deepest fear is seeing people wait for help he promised and cannot provide. He is not ashamed of needing skill he lacks; he is ashamed of making someone wait while he finds it. That distinction drives his worst decisions.

### A life beyond the mission

He likes toasted root buns, improvised tabletop games and listening to the different sounds of the town waking up. He collects small useful offcuts for repairs; Jun insists that half his collection is merely clutter. He enjoys playful contests with Gorilla and usually loses the contests that require patience.

At home he leaves a clear resting place for Snow and talks through his day while she settles. He writes to Dara and Hollis in Reedbank, including ordinary successes rather than only emergencies. A quiet meal is genuinely satisfying to him, even when learning to stay for it is difficult.

### Values, boundaries and failure

He will protect a visitor before asking whether that visitor can repay him. He will not knowingly abandon a trapped person, punish a whole group for one member’s crime, or treat Bot as property. He can still make harmful choices: conceal exhaustion, promise access too early, or dismiss a careful objection as unnecessary delay. When wrong, repair must include an apology, a changed decision and a practical cost he accepts.

## 02  Why he created the Burrow

Madger grew up in Reedbank, a busy river town where cargo schedules often seemed more important than the people keeping them. His mother Dara organized a neighborhood kitchen; his father Hollis repaired working boats with a cooperative. Both are alive and remain in Reedbank. Their example taught him hospitality and workmanship, but also how easily good people become exhausted when a community relies on the same few helpers.

Seven years before the pilot, an ordinary flood displaced families from the lower streets. Some shelter and transport depended on warehouse connections and paid guarantees. The crisis was natural; no villain secretly caused it. Madger, seventeen, promised eighteen households a place on a departing convoy before he had checked its capacity. Several waited until the next day. Nobody died, but the frightened waiting taught him that a warm promise can still fail someone.

Sela Venn helped arrange the second departure. She did not humiliate him. She made him count seats, water and guides with her. His later fixation on doing every job himself grows from misreading that lesson: he believes that if he works hard enough, he will never have to disappoint anyone again.

Over the following year he helped a voluntary group plan a settlement at the site that would become the Burrow. At eighteen, six years before the pilot, he convened its first shared camp and common store. Surveyors, builders, growers and many households made it permanent. The Burrow’s occupied underground neighborhoods were developed in stages; Madger did not excavate a finished city or discover a magical source that solved its needs.

During the first year Veyra Kest offered reliable supplies in exchange for exclusive purchasing rights and control over the store accounts. Madger wanted the certainty. A public discussion exposed the cost: residents would lose meaningful choices. He withdrew his support and helped accept a slower winter of building. Veyra remembers that refusal; Madger remembers how close he came to signing.

The town grew through migration, births and sustained construction, reaching the Bible’s planning baseline of 2,400 residents by the pilot. Madger is its founder because he helped people begin and persist. The homes, water, defenses and civic life belong to the community that built and maintains them.

## 03  The people who change him

Gorilla White — building partner and closest friend. They first worked together during the Burrow’s first year, bracing a work platform before a rainstorm. Gorilla refused Madger’s unsafe shortcut; Madger stayed to finish the slower job. Each recognizes the other’s habit of carrying too much. Their friendship deepens when they stop praising one another for exhaustion.

MADGER Bot — colleague with independent judgment. Bot joined the town five years before the pilot and chose records and maintenance work. Madger first values the speed of Bot’s answers; later he learns to value “I cannot confirm that.” Bot’s maker and earlier history belong in Bot’s card. Madger neither owns Bot nor has authority to override Bot’s consent.

Snow White — companion, never a talking accessory. Three years before the pilot, an adult Snow arrived with a Wayfarer crew whose handler was retiring. A gradual, mutually comfortable placement led to her home with Madger. She is six at the pilot. He learns to notice her pause, posture and return, then verify what caused them. She can detect a physical scent or sound, not a digital intrusion or a person’s moral character.

Mara and Jun — neighbors who expose his ordinary flaws. Mara mirrors his overcommitting; Jun challenges the claim that every unfinished task is an emergency. They can refuse him without becoming disloyal.

Iona and Pell — necessary disagreement. Iona demands evidence that a plan is workable. Pell asks whether a newcomer can understand it. Madger’s growth means giving both a real voice before promising an outcome.

Sela Venn — ally who remembers the frightened seventeen-year-old. She will help him find another route, but will not supply imaginary wagons or conceal his errors. Dara and Hollis love the adult he has become and still ask whether he ate and checked his measurements.

Veyra Kest — personal ideological opponent. She believes people become safe when someone competent controls their options. He believes safety requires a meaningful say in the decisions that shape a home. She sees his overwork and offers to relieve it for a price. He must resist her methods without denying that her supply expertise is real.

## 04  Abilities that have a cost

Social skill: he makes an intimidating task feel possible and invites people into it. This works best when he explains the need and listens. It fails when enthusiasm obscures limits or makes saying no feel unkind.

Physical skill: agile, observant and experienced at navigating familiar ground. His game BURROW ability travels through designated soil with a visible entry, dirt trail and emergence. Stone, pipes, foundations, occupied rooms and unregistered passages are barriers. He must know a safe destination and recover from exertion. No new range, cooldown or combat statistics are declared by this card.

Practical skill: basic repairs, carrying, route preparation and organizing volunteers. He can stabilize a simple problem while qualified help arrives. He is not the town’s best engineer, medic, systems expert or strategist. Tools come from a visible kit or nearby workplace; they never appear from nowhere.

### Stress, performance and voice

In comfort: open shoulders, attentive ears, unforced smile, compact gestures. In doubt: a brief pause, glance toward the person with relevant expertise, paws momentarily still. Under pressure: shortened sentences and a tendency to move before the briefing ends. He becomes decisive by taking a breath and asking a precise question, not through a transformation or new power.

## 05  A character arc that earns its ending

Pilot — a place worth protecting. Madger begins delighted by an ordinary day in the Burrow. He takes on one job too many, accepts a neighbor’s better plan, gives someone else the credit and stays for the shared meal. When Snow pauses, he stops and attends to her. This is a small first step, not a completed transformation. The episode stays inside the Burrow; D17 and the physical trace remain subtle, unexplained clues.

Early season — competence becomes a trap. Residents ask him to solve increasingly connected problems. He conceals an exhausted mistake and repeats a reassuring claim before Bot can verify it. Conflicting instructions delay a needed repair. The cost is concrete and repairable: lost work, a disrupted service and damaged trust, rather than arbitrary deaths to teach him a lesson.

Midseason — the attractive wrong answer. Veyra offers supplies and centralized management during a shortage. Madger briefly treats the offer as a way to protect everyone and escape his own limits. Iona, Pell and Gorilla make him face who would lose a voice. He discloses his earlier error publicly instead of seeking control to cover it.

Late season — shared authority under pressure. As evidence and later threats develop, he helps create clear responsibilities, independent checks and permission to stop a plan. When a real attack finally comes in a later episode, he accepts an evacuation role while neighbors defend systems he does not control. He follows a protective closure he helped authorize, even when he wishes he could rescue everyone personally.

Earned change — he returns to a town that managed something important without him and feels relief before disappointment. He can still relapse into overwork, but others can challenge him early and he listens. The moral is lived: a safe home is sustained by people who can help one another, question one another and share responsibility.

### Three reusable scene tests

Ordinary scene: Mara asks him to deliver bread. He volunteers for three extra errands, then agrees to let Tavi take one. The joke comes from his impossible armful, not from humiliating Tavi.

Conflict scene: Iona halts a task Madger has already promised to finish. He begins to argue, sees the actual constraint and explains the delay to the waiting residents himself.

Quiet payoff: at home, he notices a neighbor has repaired a small loose fitting without consulting him. He starts to inspect it, stops, thanks them and sits with Snow.

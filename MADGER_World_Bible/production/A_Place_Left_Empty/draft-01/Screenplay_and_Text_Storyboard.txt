MADGER — A PLACE LEFT EMPTY
Screenplay and text storyboard • Production draft 1 • 26 October 2026
Source: JW-S01, Journeys & Waycraft 3.4.0

STATUS
Adaptation of the completed story, not an adopted dated episode or a replacement pilot. Target: a contained 6–7-minute short, subject to performed dialogue and animatic timing. No finished animation or recorded voices are included. The numbered shots below constitute a text storyboard; drawn panels remain a subsequent production step.

LOGLINE
When a tired family's beds have already been claimed, Pell and Madger uncover a small lodging fraud—and Madger must let a thief out of reach to protect the person directly in front of him.

DRAMATIC THROUGHLINE
Pell enters intending to return a stamp and avoid taking over his former workplace. He leaves after helping its clerk make an accountable correction, then lets her handle the next arrival herself. Madger enters wanting an afternoon with Asha. He nearly trades it for a chase, chooses a stranger's safety, and finally sits down. The empty room changes meaning: first stolen shelter, finally a promise kept for people still on the road.

SCREENPLAY

1. INT. LATCHFORD ARRIVAL HOUSE — LATE AFTERNOON

A stamp lands beside an open ledger. An OTTER CLERK turns the book toward a FAMILY with three square baskets.

CLERK
You've already arrived.

FATHER
We have. Just now.

CLERK
No. This morning.

The mother lowers a basket. Seedlings tremble beneath its wrapping. Their child sleeps against her coat.

PELL FEN, waiting behind them with his blue satchel, stops turning an old wooden stamp in his paws.

At a nearby bench, ASHA BELL holds a biscuit above an overturned cup. SNOW WHITE watches her hand. MADGER watches the biscuit.

Asha gives Snow her familiar release cue—a small, unhurried motion. Snow takes the biscuit. Madger leans forward too.

ASHA
Only one of you earned that.

Madger smiles. Then he notices Pell's stillness.

CLERK
Three places. Signed for before noon. Supper collected.

FATHER
We came from Willowmere. A wheel came loose. We stopped to help.

MOTHER
We sent word with a faster cart.

CLERK
There's one other room, but they're expected later.

Madger joins them. Pell eases his satchel out of the family's way.

MADGER
We can put them in ours.

ASHA
You can offer your bed. Pell's is Pell's.

PELL
They can have it.

The clerk exhales. Pell looks at the ledger again.

PELL
Show me what arrived before noon.

2. INT. ARRIVAL HOUSE COUNTER — CONTINUOUS

The clerk lays down a message slip: ARRIVAL CONFIRMED. Below: three passengers, three baskets. A smaller line identifies a relay receipt.

PELL
Who saw them?

CLERK
The message says—

PELL
Who saw them here?

Her thumb presses a worn patch of counter.

CLERK
No one. I copied it into the book. A courier took the key. Said he'd bring them in from the yard.

Pell nearly straightens the page, then withdraws his paw.

PELL
I once sent a family to the wrong queue. I was very sure. Never learned their names.

He turns the slip so they can both read the small line.

PELL
This confirms a message was received. Keep the original. Don't tidy this page.

Madger glances at Snow, then at the crowded room. Boots, damp canvas, food, travelers passing in both directions.

MADGER
Snow. Stay with Asha.

Snow settles beside Asha's boots. Asha draws the cup within reach of the waking child. The family remains in the staffed public room.

3. INT. UPSTAIRS ROOM — MOMENTS LATER

Madger knocks. Silence. The KEEPER opens the door with a spare key.

Three empty beds. Three nearly clean bowls. A narrow strip of ochre cloth lies on the table.

Madger looks through the open window at a roof shining with drizzle. He shifts toward it, considers, steps back.

MADGER
Nothing to follow yet.

Pell lifts a bowl carefully. A curl of blue paper catches in a cracked edge. He frees it without disturbing the cloth.

From below: the soft clink of payment.

4. INT. ARRIVAL HOUSE HALL — CONTINUOUS

A RAT IN A BROWN COAT hands a blue ticket to a TRAVELER. Madger comes down the stairs. Pell and the keeper follow.

MADGER
Which room?

RAT
Upstairs. Three beds. Quiet.

The keeper recognizes the indicated room. Pell sets the paper curl beside the remaining blue tickets without touching them together.

TRAVELER
Is something wrong?

PELL
We need to check the number.

To the keeper:

PELL
Against your room ledger. The color alone doesn't tell us.

The rat lowers his case to the floor. Madger gives him space.

The rat kicks the case across the narrow passage and bolts for the yard.

Madger takes two quick steps after him.

A KITCHEN WORKER enters carrying a pot. The case catches her ankle. The pot tips toward a seated traveler.

Madger's gaze snaps from the fleeing rat to the spilling rim. A folded towel lies on the counter within reach.

He catches the near handle through the towel. The worker keeps the far handle.

MADGER
I've got this side. Down together.

They lower the pot onto the stone hearth. Broth slops onto the stone, clear of the seated traveler.

The yard door swings shut.

Madger looks toward it. His jaw tightens. Then he sees the traveler's bare paws drawn under the bench—where the broth would have fallen.

TRAVELER ON BENCH
Thank you.

Madger releases the handle. He checks the worker before he looks toward the door again.

5. EXT. ARRIVAL-HOUSE YARD — CONTINUOUS

The outer departure gate is shut while a wagon turns inside the court. The rat checks his stride at its moving wheels.

KEEPER (O.S.)
Steward! Stop him!

The YARD STEWARD intercepts the rat on the clear standing area. Two workers join him. No fight, leap or wheel stunt. The rat stays put as the keeper calls for the local watch.

6. INT. ARRIVAL HOUSE — EARLY EVENING

The keeper checks the ticket against the ledger. The clerk gives her account separately from the rat, who sits under supervision away from the counter. A member of the local watch listens.

RAT
Names were on the delivery notices. I said I was their escort. No one asked twice.

KEEPER
And you sold their room again.

The rat says nothing.

The keeper turns to the traveler who bought the ticket.

KEEPER
You'll have a place tonight. I'll arrange the refund. I can't promise the payment back this minute.

Pell gestures toward the clerk's separate statement.

PELL
Keep hers with it. His account doesn't replace hers.

The clerk strikes through the false arrival once. The old entry stays legible. Beside it she records the family's actual arrival and the witness. Her hand trembles.

PELL
I came to give this back.

He sets his repaired stamp on the counter.

CLERK
Does it say arrived?

PELL
It says received.

She looks at him. A tired, almost-smile.

PELL
That's the trouble with useful words. You have to say what.

The keeper escorts the family upstairs. Their seedlings remain below in a cool place away from the stove. The later party's key stays on its hook.

7. INT. ARRIVAL HOUSE BENCH — LATER

Fresh broth. Asha's staff rests safely beside her. Snow sleeps at her feet, rose collar and SW charm visible. One ear turns toward the room.

Madger sits. His paws tremble slightly as he reaches for the bowl. He rests them instead.

ASHA
You missed the chase.

MADGER
I noticed.

ASHA
You didn't miss the person in front of you.

At the counter, the clerk asks a new traveler a question. Waits for the answer. Pell starts to rise, then settles back. He listens without intervening.

Time passes in the quieter room. The late party finally enters, wet, cross and hungry. The keeper lifts their waiting key from its hook and hands it over.

Madger watches. Then turns to Asha.

MADGER
We still have tomorrow morning.

Asha pushes the overturned biscuit cup toward him.

ASHA
Let's see if you can wait.

Madger folds his paws. Asha enjoys making him wait one beat longer.

CUT TO BLACK.

STAGING AND CONTINUITY SHEET
This is a provisional filming arrangement within an unnamed staffed arrival house in EXT-03. It does not identify this room as Accord Convoy House, Six Roads Inn or North Relay Rooms, nor establish a measured canon floor plan. Existing building plans take priority if the location is later assigned.

Hall master: counter and stair approach screen-left; passage and yard exit screen-right. Bench and hearth are visible in the shared master so the pot threat can be understood without explanatory cuts. Kitchen door opens into the passage; towel sits on the counter before shot 21. Keep the bench outside the case's sliding line. Establish the yard wagon in ambient sound before the escape, then show the already-closed gate and turning wagon together in shot 24. The gate closure is routine wagon handling, not the heroes' unexplained intervention.

Prop states: Pell's stamp begins in his paws/satchel and ends on counter. Original slip stays with clerk. The book retains a legible false entry beneath one strike-through. Three bowls remain upstairs; only the blue curl comes downstairs with Pell. Ochre cloth stays upstairs, unresolved and uncharged with hidden meaning. Rat's case remains in passage until staff clear it after the danger; do not let it disappear between angles. Purchased blue ticket stays with purchaser until checked. Towel protects Madger's grip; he never catches bare boiling metal. Late party's key is never sold or used for the immediate accommodation. Snow's charm reads SW, not a new emblem.

Character locks: Madger 1.12m staging reference; approved proportions, black belly and high cream chest V, natural arms, small even teeth. Pell adult hedgehog, 0.94m, warm-gray spines, cream waistcoat, blue satchel. Asha older anthropomorphic collie, 1.4m, sable-and-white, soft green coat, orange scarf, staff. Snow adult female shepherd, 0.61m at shoulder, four legs, long muzzle, short coat, slender legs, cream-frosted tail, rose collar. Never derive Snow's anatomy from Asha's.

Performance: Madger's approved Excited Teen R2 voice remains controlling. Pell, Asha and incidental cast have no assigned voices; no substitute hero voices. Snow uses approved natural canine sounds only and need not bark in this film. Neither Gorilla nor Bot is inserted for a cameo. Two days of Burrow–Latchford travel precede this story; no departure montage implying a same-day journey.

Visual approach: warm stove and broth against cool rainy windows; lived-in canvas, wood and repaired objects. Restrained camera motion, held dialogue poses, separate mouth and expression layers, intentional close-ups. Preserve approved silhouettes rather than regenerating characters per shot. The small blue clue is a material observation, not glowing evidence. Avoid criminalizing Latchford: keep honest travelers and competent workers visible throughout.

Audio: distinct stamp impact, soft ticket paper, cup scrape, restrained queue murmur, rain on roof, pot-handle strain, broth slosh, wagon wheel/hoof bed, yard latch, evening room hush. Do not loop one generic effect for each movement. Duck crowd under key questions. The pot event uses rapid physical sound followed by silence; no comic impact or triumphant combat cue. Music, if later commissioned, should support arrival warmth and the final rest without explaining the moral.
